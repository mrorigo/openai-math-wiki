## Abstract

There are absolute constants $`\eta,c_1\gt 0`$ such that, for every sufficiently large integer <i>n</i>, one can choose <i>n</i> points in the unit square so that every triangle they determine has area at least $`c_1n^{-2+\eta}`$. Thus the almost <i>n</i><sup>−2</sup> upper-bound formulation of Heilbronn's triangle problem is false. The exponent <i>η</i> is fixed but extremely small.
